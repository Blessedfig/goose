    Field guide

    ガチョウ図鑑

    Goose Goose's extended family. The true geese belong to two living genera: Anser, the grey and white geese, and Branta, the black geese. A few birds called geese belong to neither.

    Canada goose

    Branta canadensis · true goose

    • 75 to 110 cm long, with a wingspan of up to 185 cm.
    • He says "honk." She says "hrink," shorter and higher. They use ten different calls in all.
    • The giant subspecies was thought extinct until 1962, when a flock was found wintering in Rochester, Minnesota.
    Wikipedia: Canada goose

    Snow goose

    Anser caerulescens · true goose

    • Comes in white and blue forms, once thought to be separate species.
    • A pink bill with black cutting edges gives it a "grin patch."
    • The lesser snow goose breeding population tops 5 million birds, and their calls carry for over a mile.
    Wikipedia: Snow goose

    Barnacle goose

    Branta leucopsis · true goose

    • Nests on cliffs up to 200 to 300 m high. On their first day, the goslings jump.
    • Named for the medieval belief that it grew from barnacles on driftwood.
    • Pope Innocent III banned eating it during Lent in 1215.
    Wikipedia: Barnacle goose

    Brant

    Branta bernicla · true goose

    • Has the shortest tail of any goose.
    • Winters on eelgrass, salt-marsh plants and algae. When a 1931 eelgrass blight nearly wiped out East Coast brant, they survived by switching to sea lettuce.
    • The North Sea's Brent oilfield is named after it.
    Wikipedia: Brant

    Emperor goose

    Anser canagicus · true goose

    • Eats mostly clams and other shellfish, earning the name "beach goose."
    • About 90% of the world's emperor geese nest on Alaska's Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta.
    • Migrates only 600 to 760 km, far less than most geese.
    Wikipedia: Emperor goose

    Bar-headed goose

    Anser indicus · true goose

    • Crosses the Himalayas, from sea level, non-stop in as little as seven hours.
    • One tracked bird reached 7,290 m.
    • A single change in its hemoglobin helps its blood grab oxygen from thin air.
    Wikipedia: Bar-headed goose

    Nene, the Hawaiian goose

    Branta sandvicensis · true goose

    • Hawaii's state bird since 1957.
    • Down to 30 birds in 1952. Bred back at Slimbridge in England under Peter Scott, it numbered 3,862 in 2022, still the world's rarest goose.
    • Its feet are less webbed than other geese's, for walking on cooled lava.
    Wikipedia: Nene

    Greylag goose

    Anser anser · true goose

    • The wild ancestor of most domestic geese.
    • Orange or pink bill and pink legs.
    • The goose Konrad Lorenz studied when he described imprinting.
    Wikipedia: Greylag goose

    Swan goose

    Anser cygnoides · true goose

    • The wild ancestor of the "Chinese goose" breeds of eastern Asia.
    • Domestic swan geese are easy to spot by the large knob at the base of the bill.
    Wikipedia: Domestic goose

    Egyptian goose

    Alopochen aegyptiaca · not a true goose

    • Most closely related to the shelducks, but it swims well and looks heavy in flight, hence the name.
    • Shown regularly in ancient Egyptian art.
    • Lives up to 15 years in the wild and has reached 35 in captivity.
    Wikipedia: Egyptian goose

    Emden goose

    Domestic breed · Goose Goose's real-life lookalike

    • Pure white with an orange bill and feet, from Emden in northwestern Germany.
    • Its roots are believed to go back to the 13th century.
    • Ganders can stand about a metre tall, and males weigh 11 to 12 kg.
    Wikipedia: Emden goose

    Teeth, technically

    The spiky rows on a goose's bill and tongue are tomia, made of cartilage rather than enamel. They grip and tear grass, roots and water plants, and barbs at the back of the tongue keep food from coming back up.

    IFLScience

    Different calls

    Canada geese use ten vocalizations, each for a different situation. A confrontation usually opens with a high-pitched hiss.

    Wikipedia: Canada goose

    The biggest wild goose

    An exceptionally large male giant Canada goose weighed 10.9 kg with a 2.24 m wingspan, the largest wild goose on record.

    Wikipedia: Canada goose

    Decides a snow goose's color

    White and blue snow geese interbreed. A single dominant gene for the dark form decides which one a gosling becomes.

    Wikipedia: Snow goose

    Ways to say back off

    Honking, hissing, wing flapping and head pumping. Goose Goose relies on a fifth: staring.

    Forest Preserve District of Will County
    Goose bumps

    Named after plucked skin

    Geese grow feathers from pores that look like human hair follicles. A plucked goose's skin is covered in little bumps, just like ours when we're cold or scared.

    Wikipedia: Goose bumps

    The life of a goose

    ガチョウの一生
    1. Pairing up

      Canada geese start looking for a mate at two or three, and pairs often stay together for a decade or more. Geese stay paired all year.

      Will County, Wikipedia
    2. Nesting

      A Canada goose lays 2 to 9 eggs, about 5 on average, and incubates them for 24 to 32 days. A pair raises one brood a year.

      Wikipedia: Canada goose
    3. Hatching

      Goslings can walk, swim and find their own food as soon as they hatch, and they bond with whatever they see first. Konrad Lorenz showed that incubator-hatched birds would follow him instead, work that shared the 1973 Nobel Prize.

      Wikipedia, Nobel Prize
    4. First flight

      Young Canada geese fly at 6 to 9 weeks, and stay with their parents until the following spring.

      Wikipedia, Will County
    5. The summer molt

      Adults molt in late June or early July and can't fly for about a month.

      Will County
    6. Migration

      Flocks fly in a V, which takes about half the energy of flying alone, swapping leaders as they tire. Geese navigate partly by Earth's magnetic field and spend up to 85% of migration resting at stopovers.

      Will County, Wikipedia
    7. A long life

      Wild Canada geese that reach adulthood live about 10 to 24 years. The oldest on record was a 33-year-old female tagged in Ohio in 1969.

      Wikipedia: Canada goose

    Geese and people

    人とガチョウ

    7,000 years of domestic geese

    The earliest evidence of domestic geese comes from the Hemudu culture on China's Lower Yangtze River, about 7,000 years ago. Egypt has archaeological evidence of domestic geese more than 4,000 years old.

    Domestic geese come from two wild birds: the greylag in Europe, northern Africa and western Asia, and the swan goose in eastern Asia. Some breeds reach 10 kg, more than double their wild ancestors.

    Wikipedia: Domestic goose

    Geese on guard duty

    Geese are loud, quick to notice unusual movement, and the gander puts himself between a threat and his family. People have put that to work:

    • Late 1950s: South Vietnam's air force used flocks to guard parked aircraft at night.
    • 1959 to 2012: the "Scotch Watch" geese patrolled Ballantine's whisky warehouse in Dumbarton, Scotland.
    • During COVID-19: about 500 geese helped patrol 533 km of China's border with Vietnam near Chongzuo.

    Wikipedia: Domestic goose, Wikipedia: Scotch Watch

    Geese in history and culture

    文化
    1. About 2570 BCE

      The Meidum Geese, six painted geese from the tomb of Nefermaat in Egypt, considered a masterpiece of Egyptian art and now in the Egyptian Museum in Cairo. They show greylag, greater white-fronted and red-breasted geese.

      Wikipedia: Meidum Geese
    2. 390 BCE

      Gauls climb Rome's Capitoline Hill in the dark. The sacred geese of Juno honk and wake the guards.

      World History Encyclopedia
    3. 1215

      Many believed barnacle geese grew from barnacles, so some clerics ate them on fast days as "not meat." Pope Innocent III banned eating them during Lent. The myth lasted until the late 1700s.

      Wikipedia: Barnacle goose
    4. 1650 and 1695

      "Mother Goose" stories turn up in French writing by 1650. Charles Perrault's fairy tales become known as Contes de ma mère l'Oye, Tales of My Mother Goose.

      Wikipedia: Mother Goose
    5. 1906–1907

      Selma Lagerlöf's The Wonderful Adventures of Nils: a shrunken boy crosses Sweden on the back of Martin, a white farm goose. Written as a geography reader for schools, it put Nils and his goose on Sweden's 20-krona note until 2015.

      Wikipedia
    6. The holidays

      Roast goose is a European Christmas favorite, a German Christmas Day staple, and traditional on St. Martin's Day.

      Wikipedia: Christmas goose
    7. 2019

      Untitled Goose Game, made by four people in Melbourne, wins Game of the Year at both the Game Developers Choice Awards and the D.I.C.E. Awards and sells over a million copies by year's end. In it, the goose must impersonate a concrete goose.

      Wikipedia, Wikipedia: Concrete goose

    Porch goose history

    玄関ガチョウの歴史

    The concrete goose, also called the porch goose or lawn goose, is an American lawn ornament with a wardrobe. Goose Goose stands in a long, proud line.

    1. Mid-1900s

      Concrete geese reportedly first appear around the middle of the century, supposedly in the upper Ohio River Valley. Nobody knows for sure.

      The Spokesman-Review / Washington Post
    2. The 1980s

      Peak goose. Porch geese spread across the Midwest, with a strong presence in Chicago. Owners dress them for holidays, for the weather, or just because.

      Wikipedia: Concrete goose, The Spokesman-Review
    3. Why a goose?

      Carla Bruni of the Chicago Bungalow Association links them to loyalty and to the story of the geese that alerted Rome. Geese have long guarded farms, so a goose by the door may be a sign of protection.

      The Spokesman-Review / Washington Post
    4. The 2020s

      The revival. Seasonal outfits flood TikTok. One retailer sells more than 60 goose outfits from $19.99 to $34.99, and the yellow raincoat remains a classic.

      Apartment Therapy, The Spokesman-Review
    5. 2025

      The Porch Goose Club of America on Facebook passes 200,000 members, who compete for porch goose of the month.

      The Spokesman-Review / Washington Post
    6. Today

      Goose Goose keeps watch over The Blessed Fig Tree in Antigo, Wisconsin, in whatever the season calls for.

    Caring for a concrete goose

    • Don't stand him straight on dirt or grass, which soak moisture into the concrete. A paver, brick or pad underneath helps.
    • Trapped water that freezes is a common cause of cracking. Start winterizing as temperatures near 50°F, and bring smaller geese into a garage, shed or basement.
    • If he winters outside, use a breathable, waterproof cover, not a tight plastic tarp that traps moisture.
    • Clean with a soft brush, mild dish soap and warm water. Skip bleach, acid cleaners and pressure washers.
    • Check him every spring and patch small cracks early. Fine hairline cracks are normal.

    Garden Artisans: caring for concrete statuary

    Goose Goose's rules for porch geese

    Not science. Just how things are done around here.

    1. Dress for the season. A bare goose on a holiday is a missed opportunity.
    2. The yellow raincoat is always correct when it rains.
    3. Glasses make any goose look well read.
    4. A goose with a broken foot deserves a comeback, not a retirement.
    5. Never ask a porch goose to move. He will not.

    Wisconsin geese

    ウィスコンシン

    Horicon Marsh

    More than 33,000 acres, one of the largest freshwater marshes in the United States, a refuge since 1927, and a Wetland of International Importance. Wisconsin's DNR estimates 100,000 to 200,000 geese stop there each fall.

    U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, WPR

    The Goose Wars

    By fall 1965, 121,000 geese stopped at Horicon, and agencies tried to scatter them with planes and airboats. Wisconsin sued the federal government in 1966. By the mid-1970s about a quarter million Canada geese gathered there, and the hazing failed: geese return to the places they know.

    Wisconsin Public Radio

    The giant returns

    The giant Canada goose was thought extinct in the 1950s, until Harold Hanson found a small flock wintering in Rochester, Minnesota, in 1962.

    Wikipedia: Canada goose

    Goose questions

    よくある質問
    Do geese have teeth?

    Not real ones. The serrated edges of the bill and the spikes on the tongue are tomia, made of cartilage, used for gripping and tearing plants. IFLScience

    Do geese mate for life?

    Geese form pairs that stay together all year, and Canada goose pairs often last a decade or more. If one dies, the other finds a new mate. Will County

    Why do geese fly in a V?

    It saves energy, about half compared with flying alone according to studies, and helps the flock keep track of each other. The lead bird drops back when it tires; in families, the parents usually lead. Will County, Wikipedia

    Why do geese honk?

    To call to each other and to warn. In Canada geese the male's call is the honk and the female's is a shorter, higher "hrink," and they have ten calls in all. Wikipedia

    What do geese eat?

    Canada geese are mainly vegetarian: grasses, grains like wheat, rice and corn, and water plants, with the odd insect or small fish. The emperor goose is the exception, living mostly on shellfish. Wikipedia: Canada goose, Emperor goose

    Is an Egyptian goose a goose?

    Not a true goose. It's most closely related to the shelducks, and only got the name because it swims well and looks heavy in flight. Wikipedia

    Are geese really good guards?

    Ask Rome. Geese are loud, aggressive toward threats and alert to unusual movement, and they've guarded aircraft, a whisky warehouse and a national border. Wikipedia

    Why is a porch goose always dressed up?

    Owners dress them for holidays, for the weather, or purely as decoration. It's been part of the tradition since the 1980s. Wikipedia
